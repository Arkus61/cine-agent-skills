Use this catalog to generate candidates, not to force a template. A candidate earns selection only through observable fit with the concept.

## Dramatic spines

| Model | Best fit and useful component | Risk | Anti-pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Three-Act | A causal work needs legible establishment, sustained development, and consequence. Use turns as changes in available action, not page percentages. | The middle becomes an undifferentiated holding area; short or associative work gets padded. | Renaming every opening, middle, and ending as three acts without showing different event logic. |
| Five-Act | Stage-like, broadcast, or densely turning drama benefits from more pressure chambers, act-outs, or a central high point. | Frequent partitions fragment quiet escalation or are mistaken for broadcaster ad breaks that were never supplied. | Adding five headings to a three-act outline while events remain unchanged. |
| Eight-Sequence | A feature-scale causal line benefits from medium-length objectives with a turn or answer at each sequence boundary. | Runtime arithmetic replaces causality; short work acquires needless sections. | Treating eight equal blocks as mandatory or copying a fixed proprietary beat list. |
| Freytag | Tragic or theatrical material benefits from examining rise, central culmination, counter-movement, and consequence. | A mid-work climax and descending action do not fit every modern screen story. | Calling any triangle-shaped intensity curve universal. |
| Save the Cat beat logic | Commercially legible pacing needs a diagnostic check that setup, catalyst, choice, pressure, crisis, and resolution are doing distinct jobs. | A branded ordering and timing grid can flatten voice, genre, culture, or format. | Reproducing the proprietary fifteen-beat sheet, prescribed page percentages, or beat names as a compulsory output. |
| Kishotenketsu | Associative, observational, comic, lyrical, or contrast-driven work benefits from introduction, development, recontextualizing turn, and integration without requiring conflict as the engine. | “Twist” is reduced to a gimmick or the model is exoticized as one universal East Asian form. | Forcing an antagonist into a contrast-based design, or claiming that the model forbids all conflict. |

## Journey and cycle models

| Model | Best fit and useful component | Risk | Anti-pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero's Journey | A protagonist crosses into unfamiliar conditions, faces trials, changes, and brings consequence or knowledge back to a community. It can diagnose threshold and return functions. | The protagonist becomes passive before a checklist; a return, mentor, ordeal, or lone hero is imposed where none fits. | Treating a comparative myth pattern as a universal screenplay formula or using a protagonist's gender to select it. |
| Heroine's Journey | A story specifically examines separation from devalued relational or culturally coded qualities, an externally validated identity, descent, reconnection, and integration. | Essentializing women, assigning the model by gender, or translating a psycho-spiritual model into generic “strong female hero” beats. | Using it without concept evidence about the particular split, value system, wound, and integration at stake. |
| Story Circle | A compact character-need cycle can track movement from a stable condition into unfamiliar pressure, costly adaptation, and a changed relation to the starting condition. | It privileges return/change even when the work is static, open, collective, or noncausal. | Copying a remembered eight-step chant instead of testing whether need, cost, return, and change exist. |
| Circular | The ending materially returns to an opening place, image, action, condition, or question with changed meaning. | Repetition substitutes for development; closure becomes over-neat. | Calling a repeated image circular when nothing in its meaning or context changes. |

## Arrangement and distribution models

| Model | Best fit and useful component | Risk | Anti-pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Episodic | A renewable premise supports discrete local objectives, encounters, cases, performances, or observations with variable serial accumulation. | Units reset so completely that consequence and progression disappear. | Requiring one season-long quest or identical hooks in every unit. |
| Nonlinear | Reordering chronology creates a specific curiosity, comparison, memory, causality, or subjectivity effect. | Confusion is mistaken for complexity; reveals depend on arbitrary concealment. | Moving scenes out of order when chronological order would create the same effect more clearly. |
| Anthology | Separate stories, subjects, times, or protagonists share a declared theme, world, frame, question, or formal rule. | The linking principle is too weak to create a whole. | Calling unrelated pieces an anthology without an observable curatorial relationship. |
| Frame | A presentational story, testimony, performance, archive, trial, journey, or storyteller contains and changes how embedded material is understood. | The frame merely delays entry into the main story or repeats information. | Adding a narrator because exposition is difficult rather than because the frame has agency or interpretive consequence. |
| Ensemble | Several consequential characters share a dramatic arena; attention can rotate while their choices alter one another. | Coverage becomes equal-time accounting and no relationship drives change. | Treating a group cast as one plotline or assigning every member the same structural weight. |
| Multi-protagonist | More than one protagonist carries a distinct objective and change track, with convergence, collision, relay, or deliberate separation. | One nominal protagonist remains structurally passive; objectives do not interact. | Counting viewpoint characters instead of testing who makes consequential choices. |
| Parallel | Two or more lines advance through comparison, contrast, simultaneity, cause/effect, thematic rhyme, or convergence. | Cross-cutting obscures rather than sharpens relationships between lines. | Alternating tracks on a timer with no meaningful echo, collision, or information gain. |

Ensemble, multi-protagonist, and parallel are not synonyms. Ensemble describes the distribution of consequential roles; multi-protagonist describes agency; parallel describes arrangement. They can combine, but each component needs a separate reason.

## Comparison method

1. Define shared criteria before naming a winner.
2. Select two or three candidates from different plausible logics, not cosmetic renamings of the same act spine.
3. For each candidate, state the evidence of fit, what it makes easier, and what it might distort.
4. Choose the smallest model or compatible hybrid that explains the event design.
5. Name omissions and deviations. A deliberate absence is a structural decision when tied to the brief.

Reject a model when it requires unsupported facts, a false protagonist, an unavailable runtime, a predetermined nonfiction outcome, an unwanted linear chronology, or an audience behavior claim the concept does not establish.

## Source boundaries

- John Reich's openly licensed film text distinguishes story action from plot arrangement. This reference extends that distinction with original comparison language.
- Aristotle and Freytag are used as historical analytical sources through public-domain editions; neither is treated as a universal modern screenplay prescription.
- The Joseph Campbell Foundation and Maureen Murdock's public pages establish that the Hero's and Heroine's journeys are distinct frameworks. No stage list or book text is reproduced.
- Public pages for Story Circle and Save the Cat establish the existence and broad purpose of those named models. Their ordered tutorials, beat sheets, examples, and timing rules are not copied.
- The basic four-part identification of Kishotenketsu is drawn from a CC BY-SA reference; the fit/risk guidance above is an original operational paraphrase.
