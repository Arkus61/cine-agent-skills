# Genre and Format Routing

Route from observable concept evidence. Genre supplies expectations and event pressures; format supplies scale, recurrence, closure, and presentation constraints. Neither supplies a compulsory formula.

## Selection criteria

| Criterion | Observable evidence | Structural consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Format | Exact `project_format` | Determines whole-work versus renewable-unit logic, not an act count |
| Runtime intent | Supplied duration, episode range, or unresolved runtime | Limits event density and number of independent turns; never invent a duration |
| Audience promise | Intended experience stated in the concept | Prioritizes clarity, mystery, accumulation, spectacle, emotion, rhythm, argument, or reflection |
| Genre | Declared genre signals | Suggests kinds of questions and events; conventions remain optional |
| Protagonist focus | Individual, group, place, institution, process, inquiry, or performance | Routes journey, ensemble, multi-protagonist, frame, evidence, or associative models |
| Causal engine | Objectives, opposition, investigation, contrast, rhythm, testimony, transformation, or demonstration | Selects causal spine, inquiry, associative, episodic, or performance structure |
| Chronology | Linear, memory-led, parallel time, testimony, archive, or unknown | Determines whether nonlinear devices create a named information effect |
| Closure | Complete resolution, partial answer, recurrence, open question, or serial continuation | Controls payoff and hook placement |
| Evidence status | Fictional canon, verified nonfiction fact, allegation, access, reconstruction, or unknown | Prevents invented outcomes and marks planned versus discoverable events |
| Production mode | Live action, animation, AI, or hybrid | May affect feasibility, but does not select a dramatic model by itself |

## Format routing

### Feature

Compare causal spines such as Three-Act, Five-Act, Eight-Sequence, journey/cycle, Freytag, Kishotenketsu, nonlinear, ensemble, or parallel according to the concept. A feature can be episodic or anthology-like when a coherent whole exists. Do not assume a midpoint, transformation, or return.

Observable check: can the selected model explain how one complete work changes state and pays off its audience promise?

### Short

Prefer one dominant movement, contrast, dilemma, reveal, escalation, circular return, compact act spine, or Kishotenketsu-like recontextualization. Compare at least two options even when the event count is small. Avoid miniature-feature padding.

Observable check: does every event materially orient, develop, turn, or resolve the central effect within the stated runtime?

### Series

Compare episodic, serial, braided parallel, ensemble, multi-protagonist, anthology, frame, or a macro dramatic spine. Separate renewable unit logic from cumulative change. A later season specialist owns the episode map; this artifact defines only project-level plotlines, sequences, and events.

Observable check: what can recur, what accumulates irreversibly, and what kind of closure can each unit and the larger work deliver?

### Documentary

Compare inquiry, chronology, process, place, character-led, testimony frame, essay, parallel evidence, or observational/episodic arrangements. Planned encounters are not known outcomes. Preserve access gaps, disputed claims, and discoverable material in uncertainties.

Observable check: does the arrangement make evidence status and the difference between suspense, curiosity, and unknown outcome legible without inventing a hero or villain?

### Commercial

Compare compact problem/response/payoff, demonstration, contrast, testimonial/frame, transformation, or associative brand-image logic. For a 45-second spot, event economy and time-to-orientation outweigh a feature act map. Claims must come from the concept; structure does not invent product performance.

Observable check: can a viewer identify the subject and intended experience early enough, and does the final event deliver the promised communication effect?

### Music video

Compare performance escalation, visual motif cycle, parallel performance/narrative, transformation, anthology/tableau, nonlinear association, or a compact causal story. Music sections, lyrical shifts, choreography, energy, and image recurrence can be structural evidence. A protagonist quest is optional.

Observable check: do event changes correspond to audible, performative, choreographic, visual, or narrative changes rather than invented dialogue scenes?

### Short-form

Compare demonstration, question/answer, list escalation, transformation, reveal, loop, micro-story, or direct-address frame. Orientation, novelty, legibility, and payoff must fit the stated duration. Retention is not synonymous with withholding.

Observable check: does the first event establish a reason to continue and does the last event fulfill or deliberately transform that reason?

## Genre and event routing

| Genre or event promise | Candidate logic | Useful event evidence | Formula risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mystery | Inquiry, nonlinear curiosity, frame testimony, parallel evidence | Fair clues, delayed answers, reveals that reinterpret prior facts, bounded red herrings | Concealing facts the viewpoint already knows; twist-only plotting |
| Thriller | Causal escalation, ticking-clock spine, parallel action, Five-Act pressure chambers | Shrinking options, deadlines, pursuit, dilemmas, reversals | Constant intensity without changed stakes or strategy |
| Horror | Escalation, siege, discovery, subjective/unreliable, circular contamination | Rule discovery, safety loss, dread, false security, irreversible exposure | Random shocks and victims without causal learning |
| Comedy | Escalating pattern, misunderstanding, parallel collision, Kishotenketsu contrast | Rule of repetition with variation, status change, reveal, consequence | Beat-sheet solemnity or repetition without variation |
| Romance | Dual or parallel change tracks, ensemble pressure, circular return, compact act spine | Choice, vulnerability, changed relationship terms, earned union or separation | Treating attraction as automatic agency loss or compulsory union |
| Adventure | Journey, episodic trials, Eight-Sequence objectives, ensemble quest | Thresholds, discoveries, changing plans, costs, consequence | Travel stops that do not alter objective, knowledge, or relationships |
| Tragedy | Freytag, Five-Act, causal dilemma, circular irony | Choice under pressure, reversal, recognition, consequence | Misfortune without agency or treating death as automatic tragedy |
| Slice-of-life or contemplative | Episodic, circular, Kishotenketsu, parallel observation | Accumulated detail, contrast, recontextualization, modest change | Importing an antagonist or fake crisis to “make it dramatic” |
| Performance | Energy escalation, motif cycle, parallel stage/backstage, transformation | Musical/choreographic sections, image variation, performer relation to camera | Adding a causal subplot that competes with the song or act |
| Investigation/nonfiction | Inquiry, chronology, testimony frame, parallel evidence | Sources, access, contradictions, verification, revised hypothesis | Predetermining the answer or turning allegation into event fact |

Genres can combine. When they do, name which plotline or sequence carries which promise and identify collisions—for example, mystery requires controlled information while comedy may benefit from dramatic irony. Do not make every event serve every genre.

## Hybrid and deviation tests

A hybrid is justified only when:

1. Each source model is among the two or three compared candidates.
2. Each component solves a separately stated criterion.
3. Components operate at compatible scales or their scale difference is explicit, such as an episodic unit engine inside a parallel series architecture.
4. The result can be described through observable event behavior, not model-name accumulation.
5. Removing a component would create a named loss.

An intentional deviation is required even when a model fits. State what is omitted, moved, distributed, compressed, expanded, or left open and connect it to evidence. Examples include no literal return, distributed mentor function, no fixed midpoint, open documentary outcome, resolution without a cliffhanger, performance rather than causal climax, or a contrast turn without antagonist escalation.

Reject a hybrid that uses three model names to justify one ordinary causal arc. Reject a deviation that merely says the story is “unique.”
