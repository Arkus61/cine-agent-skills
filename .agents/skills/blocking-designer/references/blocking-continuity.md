# Blocking and Continuity

## Primary axis

Define the relationship that matters most: two characters, a character and an exit, a vehicle path, or a line of action. Camera positions on one side preserve consistent screen direction. Crossing the axis can work when the move is shown, a neutral shot resets orientation, or disorientation is intentional.

## Eyelines

Record where each character looks and the approximate vertical relationship. Matching eyelines is more than left versus right; height, distance, and emotional avoidance matter.

## Motivated movement

Movement may arise from:

- acquiring or protecting an object;
- closing or creating distance;
- gaining access to information;
- preventing escape;
- performing a necessary task;
- crossing a psychological threshold.

If none applies, consider stillness.

## Reset and coverage

Flag props, doors, liquids, wardrobe changes, marks, and background actions that require reset between takes. A beautiful block that cannot be repeated is not production-ready.
