# Continuity, discontinuity, and montage

Continuity makes time, space, action, and attention easy to follow. Discontinuity makes a break perceptible. Montage creates meaning, duration, or comparison through assembly. Select each as a dramatic method, not as a quality ranking.

## Continuity controls

- Preserve screen direction, eyelines, axis, movement completion, action state, prop and wardrobe state, dialogue sense, ambience, and exposure only when supplied plans or inspected media establish them.
- Establish geography before compressing action when clarity is the dramatic priority.
- Cut on movement to carry energy across angles, but verify phase and direction from reviewed media before claiming a match.
- Use a cutaway to add supplied context or bridge a real omission. It cannot hide missing coverage if no cutaway exists.
- Use a reaction when the response changes interpretation. The reaction must answer the exact preceding event, not a convenient but mismatched take.

## Intentional discontinuity

A jump cut, axis break, graphic rupture, or repeated action can express instability, urgency, comedy, subjectivity, fragmentation, or self-conscious form. State the intended audience effect and how viewers will remain oriented. Accidental mismatch is not intentional discontinuity merely because it is noticeable.

## Adjoining images create meaning

The Kuleshov principle is operational: the shot before and after an expression changes how viewers interpret it. Associative montage extends that relation across images, sounds, motifs, or actions. Record the proposed interpretation, keep documentary claims tied to sources, and avoid treating juxtaposition as proof of causality.

## Technique map

| Technique | Primary relationship | Failure check |
|---|---|---|
| Match cut | Graphic, compositional, or movement similarity links shots. | Does the match clarify an idea rather than advertise technique? |
| Jump cut | A visible temporal or spatial skip within related material. | Is the rupture intended and still comprehensible? |
| Cutaway | A brief departure supplies context, concealment, or emphasis before return. | Is the insert actually registered? |
| Reaction cut | Consequence or interpretation appears on a subject. | Does the reaction correspond to the source event? |
| J-cut | Incoming sound begins before its image. | Does anticipation or orientation justify the advance? |
| L-cut | Outgoing sound continues over the next image. | Does continuation preserve thought, reaction, or space? |
| Sound bridge | Shared or continuous sound binds an image or time change. | Is it sourced now or explicitly planned for sound post? |
| Parallel or cross-cutting | Two lines alternate to compare, synchronize, or create dramatic irony. | Are simultaneity and geography established or intentionally uncertain? |
| Ellipsis | Omitted action advances story time. | Can viewers infer the missing interval without a false factual claim? |
| Temporal compression | Selected moments condense a process or duration. | Are essential cause, context, and consequence retained? |
| Temporal expansion | Repetition, overlap, or added viewpoints prolong an instant. | Does added screen time deepen perception rather than duplicate information? |

Montage is not synonymous with any fast sequence. It may be measured, lyrical, argumentative, comic, informational, or abrasive. Define the relationship between units and the change created by their order.
