# Editorial rhythm by format

Rhythm comes from changes in information, emotion, movement, sound, and expectation. Shot length is an outcome of those changes, not a universal target. Describe cut cues as events until media is inspected.

| Format or mode | Rhythm priority | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Dialogue | Let listening, thought, interruption, and changed tactics determine cuts; sound overlaps can preserve conversational flow. | Cutting on every line and removing meaningful pauses or reactions. |
| Comedy | Protect setup, recognition, reaction, and consequence; a hold can be as important as a fast cut. | Cutting before the audience reads the premise or after the response has gone flat. |
| Action | Alternate impact with orientation; preserve direction, objective, obstacle, and consequence across acceleration. | Equating speed with excitement until geography and stakes disappear. |
| Horror | Control what is known, withheld, anticipated, and confirmed; contrast sustained observation with selected ruptures. | Telegraphing every scare or hiding the decisive event beyond comprehension. |
| Documentary | Preserve provenance, qualification, participant context, evidentiary gaps, and ethical meaning while compressing repetition. | Manufacturing causality through juxtaposition or using unsupported illustrative coverage. |
| Music video | Choose performance, narrative, conceptual, or hybrid organization; relate image changes to phrasing, texture, motif, movement, and lyrical or dramatic turns. | Cutting on every beat with no escalation, contrast, or visual development. |
| Commercial | Establish audience tension, proposition, proof, identity, and action within the declared runtime; reserve enough attention for required comprehension. | Decorative momentum that obscures the claim, product, or call to action. |
| Short-form | Orient immediately, create legible retention turns, and deliver the promised payoff at the intended scale. | Treating confusion as a hook or repeating context until the payoff has no room. |

## Sequence shaping

1. Mark the supplied dramatic or informational turns.
2. Decide which turn needs anticipation, impact, reaction, or recovery.
3. Vary density deliberately: constant acceleration becomes a new monotony.
4. Let sound and silence create phrasing across picture cuts.
5. Review the sequence for comprehension, emotional trajectory, and unintended claims before refining micro-timing.

For a 30-second piece, account for the full declared runtime as structure, but do not invent frame-accurate durations. A beat map can use relative phases such as orientation, development, turn, payoff, and end state until media metadata is supplied.
