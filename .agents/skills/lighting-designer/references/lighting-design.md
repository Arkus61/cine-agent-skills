# Lighting Design Reference

## Motivation before equipment

Start with the story-world reason for light: window, practical, work light, display, fire, moon, vehicle, or an explicitly stylized source. A motivated source may be realized by off-screen equipment, but the plan should keep motivation and implementation distinct.

## Environment and time basis

State the location condition and scripted time that each setup must preserve: interior or exterior, day or night, elapsed story time, weather when supplied, and which environmental sources are present or withheld. Keep unconfirmed survey conditions labeled as assumptions. This basis makes matches across coverage explicit without pretending that changing natural light will remain stable on its own.

## Direction and quality

Direction determines which planes receive or lose light and whether geography remains readable. Quality describes the transition between light and shadow. Source size relative to the subject, distance, diffusion, bounce, flags, and environment all affect that transition.

Use key, fill, backlight, negative fill, practicals, and ambience as functional relationships, not mandatory ingredients.

## Color and exposure intent

Define color by relationship: neutral, warmer or cooler than another source, saturated or restrained, stable or changing. Name exact spectral or color-temperature targets only when measurement and equipment are confirmed.

Describe exposure intent through retained information: face detail, highlight headroom, readable blacks, silhouette, practical brightness, or a planned transition. Final values require the selected camera, lens, filtration, frame rate, shutter, display transform, performer, wardrobe, and location.

## Control and continuity

Record what must be shaped, dimmed, skirted, flagged, switched, matched, photographed, measured, or reset. Check the full blocking and camera path, reflective surfaces, monitor or VFX plates, focus travel, and edit continuity.

## Safety boundary

The plan identifies hazards and approval needs; it is not an electrical or rigging instruction. Qualified crew must approve power, load, mounting, overhead work, heat, cables, weather protection, emergency access, flicker or pulse exposure, and performer proximity.

Source provenance and usage limits are recorded in `docs/source-register.md`.
