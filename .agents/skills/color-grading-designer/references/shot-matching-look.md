# Balance, matching, and look

Build the result in separable decisions:

1. Identify source encodings and normalize each source into the declared or proposed working environment.
2. Balance exposure and neutral/color bias per segment using available imagery and scopes; without media, describe the review action rather than a result.
3. Match shared anchors across a cut: faces or character materials, neutrals, important production colors, shadow density, highlight behavior, and environmental continuity.
4. Preserve motivated differences such as sun versus shade, practical-light zones, or an intended time transition. Matching means coherent continuity, not identical histograms.
5. Apply the desired look after technical balance. State contrast shape, saturation distribution, palette relationships, and highlight/shadow priorities in perceptual terms unless measurements are supplied.
6. Use selective treatments only for a stated purpose. Protect skin, wardrobe, product, or character recognition colors from global moves; inspect edges, texture, noise, gamut, and temporal stability.

For VFX, verify render and camera-source encodings independently. Review edge integration, premultiplication contamination, grain/noise, black level, highlight response, and recognition-critical colors on the exact graded version.

Candidate and approval decisions are version-specific. Inspect the complete linked segment set in sequence and on the declared display path; do not inherit approval from a prior render or from creative intent.
