# Color story arc

Describe how color supports the unit's emotional progression, then make each item serve that progression. Useful dimensions include warm/cool relationships, saturation restraint or release, contrast and openness, separation from the environment, and the recurrence or transformation of motif colors.

Do not reduce an arc to palette tags. For each transition, state what changes, what remains stable, why the change belongs to the story, and which recognition-critical colors are protected. A warm reveal can retain cool shadows; a threatening passage can deepen contrast without erasing facial or material detail.

Treat skin tone, character features, costumes, brand/product colors, and established world colors as explicit protection responsibilities when relevant. Describe selective trims as conditional actions until image inspection supports their necessity and extent.

Review the arc in sequence, not only as isolated frames. Look for unintended hue drift, saturation jumps, crushed or clipped detail, unstable selections, and VFX mismatches. Display-specific trims refine a reviewed master for a declared destination; they do not silently redefine the creative master or certify an unsupported output.
