# Production sound reasoning

Use this reference to convert story intent into observable sound requirements. It is not an equipment prescription.

## Perspective before decoration

Decide what the audience can hear, how close it feels, and whether the sound belongs to the character, room, or world beyond frame. Dolby's sound-design discussions emphasize that off-screen space and subjective experience can be built through sound. Therefore, describe perspective and dramatic function before suggesting layers.

Source: [Dolby Creator Lab — Sound Design for Indie Movies](https://www.dolby.com/en-in/creator-lab/dolby-creator-talks/episode-73/).

## Production capture

For each shot, identify what dialogue, action, practical sound, room state, or timing reference must survive photography. A useful requirement describes the result — intelligible restrained dialogue during a move, a clean chamber release, or matched room tone across visual-effects passes — without inventing a microphone or recorder package.

Microphone choice and placement change the balance of direct voice, room sound, clothing noise, movement freedom, and off-axis coloration. Shure's educational guidance also stresses placement stability, strain relief, wardrobe coordination, and avoiding skin-irritating attachment methods. Treat these as survey and department decisions, not scene facts.

Source: [Shure — Audio Systems Guide for Theater Performances](https://www.shure.com/en-US/docs/education/audio-systems-guide-for-theater-performances).

## Cue categories

- `dialogue`: spoken, vocal, or breath performance that must remain intelligible and emotionally specific.
- `effects`: action, object, mechanism, body, or environmental event.
- `ambience`: the stable or evolving acoustic bed that establishes space.
- `silence`: intentional removal or withholding; specify what remains so it is executable.
- `transition`: a perspective or state change between spaces, moments, or beats.
- `music-intent`: dramatic function only, including an explicit decision to use no music.

## Continuity and restraint

Sound continuity includes practical power states, background behavior, body and costume movement, timing, and acoustic perspective. Preserve ambiguity by subtracting or withholding when appropriate; do not add a cue that objectively confirms what the image refuses to confirm.

Record fixed technical values only when supplied by production. Otherwise state the needed behavior and the approval or test that will determine implementation.
