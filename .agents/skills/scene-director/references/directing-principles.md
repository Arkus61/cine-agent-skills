# Directing Principles

## Unification

A directing concept is useful when performance, staging, camera, sound, and rhythm can all express the same dramatic idea. Reject choices that are individually attractive but point in conflicting directions.

## Point of view

Point of view is not merely whose close-up appears most often. It determines what the audience knows, when they know it, whose changes they track, and what remains outside certainty.

## Actor direction

Prefer action verbs with a target:

- make her reveal the truth;
- keep him from leaving;
- test whether she remembers;
- hide fear by controlling the procedure.

Add circumstance and obstacle. Avoid results such as “more emotional.”

## Rhythm

Rhythm comes from changing pressure. Consider sentence length, silence, overlap, physical tasks, distance, withheld reactions, and the timing of new information. Constant speed is rarely dramatic rhythm.
