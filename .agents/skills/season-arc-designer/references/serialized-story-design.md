# Serialized Story Design

Use this guide to choose episode-scale closure and season-scale accumulation from the supplied series engine. Do not treat serialization, act breaks, mythology, or cliffhangers as universal quality markers.

## Episodic and serialized balance

| Strategy | Unit responsibility | Season accumulation | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mostly episodic | Deliver a complete case, task, encounter, performance, or local dilemma. | Let choices, knowledge, competence, access, and relationships accumulate selectively. | Total reset makes order irrelevant. |
| Braided | Resolve one plot while advancing or complicating another. | Rotate A/B/C emphasis so no strand is mechanically repeated. | Every plot receives equal time regardless of need. |
| Mostly serialized | Make one consequential move, answer, reversal, cost, or strategic change legible per episode. | Carry the changed condition forward without undoing it. | Episodes become arbitrary fragments that stop before meaning. |

Ask two separate questions: what is satisfying about this episode, and what cannot return to its pre-episode state? A procedural can retain stable institutions and still change trust or knowledge. A serialized mystery can withhold its largest answer and still close a search, test, or decision.

## Episode architecture

### A/B/C distribution

- The A plot owns the episode's main objective and resolution.
- A B plot supplies a distinct pressure, relationship, investigation, or contrast that can intersect the A plot without duplicating it.
- A C plot is optional and should be compact enough not to steal consequence from A or B.
- Rotate prominence according to season need. Do not assign all three slots merely to look complete.

### Cold open

A cold open precedes the episode's main launch and earns its place by orienting a local problem, posing a fair question, creating contrast or urgency, reframing familiar conditions, or establishing a thematic lens. Use `null` when the episode opens more clearly inside its main engine. A disconnected shock is not a hook.

### Act-out

An act-out is a pressure-changing turn placed at a real presentation boundary. It may expose evidence, close an option, reverse leverage, force a choice, change a goal, or make consequence unavoidable. Record the turn and the new pressure. Do not infer a fixed act count or page position that the brief has not supplied.

### Local resolution

Resolve the episode's promised objective at the scale the audience was invited to expect. Resolution may be success, failure, compromise, answer, decision, escape, or an intelligible transformed goal. A forward hook follows that resolution; it does not replace it.

## Bottle and mythology episodes

A bottle episode concentrates already established pressures through a credible constraint: limited location, cast, time, mobility, information, or resources. Use concentration to force disclosures, tests, choices, and relationship changes. Do not use the label as permission for a consequence-free pause.

A mythology episode materially advances the larger system, history, threat, mystery, or governing rules. It should change what characters can infer or do after the episode. Do not turn it into a lecture or assume it must abandon the renewable unit engine.

The two functions may overlap when a constrained situation exposes a larger rule, but record the dominant episode type and make both effects observable.

## Cross-episode escalation

Escalation means the next episode inherits a changed problem. Vary the dimension:

- options narrow or a prior tactic stops working;
- cost becomes personal, social, institutional, or irreversible;
- scope expands from one person to a group, place, system, or value;
- opposition learns, coordinates, or changes strategy;
- knowledge creates a harder choice rather than an easy solution;
- trust, authority, obligation, or access changes hands;
- time or resources become credibly constrained.

Track the causal bridge: because episode N changed X, episode N+1 must attempt Y under condition Z. More danger, noise, cases, clues, or attackers without a new decision surface is repetition, not escalation.

## Finale responsibility

The finale is responsible to the season actually designed. It should:

1. activate only setups declared and scheduled in a strictly earlier episode;
2. confront the dramatic question through action and consequence;
3. make accumulated character and relationship change affect the result;
4. resolve the promised local or season-scale responsibility;
5. state the cost and the durable post-finale condition;
6. distinguish deliberately unresolved threads from forgotten plotlines;
7. keep next-season seeds subordinate to this season's payoff.

An enlarged threat is not a payoff. A next-season surprise cannot substitute for answering the season's central promise. Partial or transformed answers are valid when the consequence is explicit and the unresolved portion is intentional.

## Forward hooks and cliffhangers

Every nonfinal episode needs a forward hook: a concrete changed promise that targets a strictly later declared episode. The mechanism can be a question, changed goal, deadline, dilemma, revelation, consequence, relationship shift, or strategic shift.

A cliffhanger is one optional boundary technique, not a synonym for a forward hook. Use it only when interruption or transformation at the boundary adds meaning. Vary its story value:

| Type | Useful when | Weak substitute |
|---|---|---|
| Peril | An established danger changes the next action. | Random physical jeopardy. |
| Revelation | Prepared information changes interpretation or strategy. | Unexplained novelty. |
| Dilemma | The next choice sacrifices a real value either way. | An obviously correct option. |
| Reversal | A locally earned result changes direction or meaning. | Undoing the episode. |
| Mystery | A fair gap becomes newly consequential. | Hiding basic orientation. |
| Relationship | Trust, allegiance, obligation, or intimacy changes terms. | Generic argument. |
| Strategic shift | A goal, tactic, authority, or resource changes hands. | Announcing more of the same. |

Use `null` when the episode's resolved consequence, emotional aftereffect, or anticipation already supplies retention. This series-only artifact's hook rule does not apply to non-series formats.
