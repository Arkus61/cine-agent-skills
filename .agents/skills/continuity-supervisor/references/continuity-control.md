# Continuity control

## Why preproduction continuity matters

ScreenSkills notes that scenes are commonly photographed out of story order. Script supervisors prepare continuity breakdowns, monitor action and eyelines, and maintain detailed records so shots can edit together coherently. A preproduction plan should therefore identify observable relationships before the first take, while leaving take-specific records to production.

Source: [ScreenSkills — Script supervisor](https://www.screenskills.com/job-profiles/browse/film-and-tv-drama/technical/script-supervisor-film-and-tv-drama/).

## What to track

ScreenSkills' skill checklist includes screen direction, eyelines, camera movement/composition, dialogue, action, emotion, props, costume, hair, makeup, and relevant technical data. Track only what the supplied package establishes and what affects the edit or cross-department match.

Source: [ScreenSkills — Script supervisor skills](https://www.screenskills.com/skills-checklists/scripted-film-and-tv/script-supervisor-department/script-supervisor-skills/).

## State, transition, reset

- `tracked_state`: a visible or audible fact at a precise story/action phase.
- `required_transition`: how that fact must continue or deliberately change across linked shots.
- `reset_instruction`: the concrete record or restoration needed before another take or pass.

Example: `right palm stays on the chamber edge during the turn` is a state; `the insert begins at the head angle established in the wide` is a transition; `restore the palm, shoulder, head-start, and monitor states before matched passes` is a reset.

## Severity

- `info`: useful context or a record that improves later matching.
- `warning`: a mismatch will be visible, audible, or require coordination to repair.
- `critical`: a mismatch breaks screen geography, action logic, effects alignment, editability, or a central narrative fact.

Severity is not a production risk assessment or safety approval.
