# Titles and Captions Designer

Create `titles-captions-plan.json`; do not render, transcribe, edit media, encode, upload, deliver, inspect unavailable media, clear rights, or approve for another person.

1. Copy exact project and unit ownership. Register edit segments as `<UNIT>-ED###`, locked edit versions as `<PROJECT>-EV###`, existing lock decisions as `<PROJECT>-LK###`, text sources as `<PROJECT>-TS###`, exact timing records as `<PROJECT>-TM###`, evidence as `<PROJECT>-TE###`, and items as `<UNIT>-TT###`. `###` starts at 001 and permits more digits. Every populated reference and registry record must be internally consistent, even when unused.
2. Preserve supplied title, dialogue, translation, and credit text with a source pointer. Classify text structurally as `dialogue`, `nonspeech`, `title`, or `credit`; do not infer the class from wording. Main titles, intertitles, and lower thirds use `title`; credits use `credit`; subtitles use `dialogue`; accessibility captions use `dialogue` or `nonspeech`. Resolve every populated text-source ID, including proposed text. Dialogue-derived subtitles and captions require a `supplied-dialogue` source. A proposed nonspeech accessibility caption requires an `editorial-proposal` source and remains a proposal rather than performed-audio evidence. Provenance does not prove performed speech, translation approval, billing, clearance, or rights. Keep editorial wording and localization as proposals until reviewed. Unknown names, organizations, ordering, and legal language remain unresolved.
3. Give each item a type, edit segment, text content kind and state, placement, timing intent, typography intent, readability plan, language, speaker identification, sound description, burn-in/sidecar assumption, revision, and approval state. Captions carry dialogue plus relevant speaker and nonspeech information; subtitles and accessibility captions are distinct editorial choices. Read [captions readability and accessibility](references/captions-readability-accessibility.md).
4. Use `creative-intent` when exact cue metadata is unavailable; describe rhythm without timestamp-shaped placeholders. `exact-supplied` requires a timing record for the same item and segment on a supplied locked current edit version, with a declared timebase, ordered matching frame/timecode bounds, and `output-timeline` coordinates. Source-media timecodes and a lock label alone do not establish timeline cues. Read [title design and timing](references/title-design-timing.md).
5. Plan hierarchy, rhythm, type weight/scale/spacing, and placement relative to active picture and anticipated platform UI. Test contrast across changing backgrounds. A safe-area exception needs a reason. Treat safe-area values, line length, reading speed, fonts, and delivery standards as proposals unless an applicable specification is cited.
6. Bind review and approval evidence to the exact current item content, content kind, supplied/proposed state, resolved text-source snapshot, revision, language, speaker identification, sound description, placement, timing record, segment, and edit version. Evidence carries `content_kind`, `text_state`, and `text_source` (the complete registered source object, or null for an unsourced proposal). Final approval requires both current-item review and human approval carrying the same decision value. A source path is provenance, not external authenticity; later integration owns upstream verification.
7. Validate with `python -m cine_skills validate-artifact titles-captions-plan PATH/TO/titles-captions-plan.json --root REPOSITORY_ROOT` or `cine-skills validate-artifact titles-captions-plan PATH/TO/titles-captions-plan.json --root REPOSITORY_ROOT`.

With no media or locked edit, keep exact timing and approval absent while preserving substantive creative, accessibility, localization, credits, and review plans. Record assumptions and uncertainties explicitly.
