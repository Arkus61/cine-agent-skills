# Concept, Format, and Genre

Use these distinctions to keep a concept reusable without turning it into a miniature story bible.

## Concept layers

| Layer | Function | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Premise | Establish the situation, pressure, and stakes | Do not narrate the full plot |
| Logline | Compress the concept into one legible sentence | Do not add unsupported market claims |
| Central dramatic question | Define the uncertainty that can sustain attention | Do not predetermine the answer |
| Audience promise | State the intended viewing experience | Do not claim audience research unless supplied |
| Theme | Name a tension explored through choices or evidence | Do not state a compulsory moral |
| Genre | Signal a useful family of expectations | Do not treat conventions as mandatory beats |
| Tone | Describe how the material is handled | Do not substitute adjectives for premise |

An open film-studies text distinguishes story action from the way plot arranges that action and treats theme and genre as connected but separate construction choices. Apply that distinction here: the concept identifies the durable dramatic or investigative contract; downstream structure determines the arrangement.

## Format routing

- **Feature or short:** Scale the question, world, and success criteria to one complete work. Runtime changes the permitted breadth, not the obligation to use a particular act model.
- **Series:** Identify a renewable pressure or inquiry and a durable audience promise. Do not write episodes or a season arc here.
- **Documentary:** Center an evidence-bounded subject, place, process, institution, community, or inquiry. A protagonist and transformation arc are optional.
- **Commercial:** Center the communication objective and intended experience without inventing performance claims or consumer research.
- **Music video:** Center the relationship among music, performance, image, and concept; a causal plot is optional.
- **Short-form:** Center immediate orientation, a compact promise, and a feasible payoff; do not assume serialized lore.

Production mode is independent of project format. `animation` and `ai`, for example, can coexist in one series; neither says anything about episode length or audience.

## Nonfiction evidence discipline

Realism and nonfiction still involve selection, framing, editing, and sometimes reconstruction. Therefore:

1. Separate confirmed facts from allegations, memories, leads, and creative proposals.
2. State what material is known to exist: participants, records, locations, archives, observations, or access.
3. Put missing access, disputed chronology, unverifiable claims, participant consent, and unknown outcomes in `uncertainties`.
4. Frame the dramatic question so investigation can change the answer.
5. Never invent a villain, climax, redemption, or hero arc to make nonfiction resemble fiction.
