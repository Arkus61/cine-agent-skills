# Visual Language Reference

## Frame and point of view

Choose the frame boundary before designing compositions. Aspect ratio affects how much horizontal or vertical relationship can remain visible and must be communicated consistently through production and finishing.

Define what the camera is allowed to know. A subjective scene can remain near a character without imitating literal eyesight; the important constraint is whether an angle reveals information the character and audience should not yet possess.

## Composition and angle

Composition directs attention by arranging subjects, objects, lines, depth, and empty space inside the frame. Use symmetry, imbalance, central placement, thirds, frames-within-frames, and negative space only when the arrangement expresses the beat.

Camera height and angle change the viewer's relationship to a subject. Do not assign a universal meaning such as “low equals powerful.” Record the scene-specific effect and preserve the chosen rule until a motivated turn.

## Lens behavior

Describe lens strategy through observable behavior:

- field of view and how much geography remains visible;
- camera-to-subject distance and resulting spatial relationship;
- depth separation and focus demands;
- distortion tolerance near faces or architecture;
- consistency or deliberate change across beats.

Label focal lengths as indicative when sensor format and lens package are unknown. Do not confuse optical zoom with physical camera movement.

## Palette, contrast, and texture

Build a limited palette from story facts, location, wardrobe, practical sources, and the intended value shift. Define contrast through retained or lost detail, not adjectives alone. Define texture through surfaces, atmosphere, filtration, sharpness, and highlight behavior that production can observe.

## Rules and exceptions

A useful rule identifies the beat, composition, camera height, and dramatic purpose. A useful exception identifies the precise turn that earns a break. “Make it cinematic” defines neither.

Keep motifs few enough to remain legible. List prohibited defaults to prevent later departments from adding unrelated fashionable techniques.
