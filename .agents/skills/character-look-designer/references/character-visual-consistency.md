# Character visual consistency

Use this reference to preserve identity across framing, performance, state changes, and production methods. Consistency means stable recognition plus explicitly controlled variation, not one frozen pose or expression.

## Recognition anchors

Choose the smallest supplied set that makes the subject recognizable across distance and angle: silhouette breaks, feature placement, proportion relationships, repeated surface boundaries, appendage count, or other subject-specific structure. Record each as an immutable anchor only when the source establishes it. A proposed rendering convention belongs in visual treatment, not canon.

Bind canon literally, not approximately. Every anchor or other appearance claim labelled `supplied` must repeat an exact `(value, source_reference)` pair from the source ledger for that same character. A similar sentence, a reference without the exact value, or a pair declared for another character cannot establish canon.

Test anchors at full figure, medium view, close detail, profile, and partial occlusion as required by supplied shots. Avoid false precision: if exact ratios or measurements are absent, state the relationship and request a reference decision.

## Controlled variation

Separate four layers:

1. Immutable identity anchors never change.
2. Permitted variation defines the allowed range for pose, expression, articulation, grooming, deformation, or stylization.
3. Continuity states record story-ordered wardrobe, surface, light, contamination, wear, or damage changes.
4. Shot presentation changes framing and visibility without redefining the subject.

A state may preserve an anchor or vary a declared non-anchor. It cannot contradict, mirror, relocate, remove, duplicate, or replace an immutable anchor.

## Views and turnaround coverage

Request only views required to resolve the supplied coverage and known drift risks. Typical needs include front and back turnaround, left or right profile, three-quarter view, feature detail, expression sheet, pose sheet, wardrobe state, and material/wear state. For asymmetric features, include the side needed to establish placement and an opposing view that prevents accidental mirroring.

Every shot coverage record links to a state and at least one reference. Every reference links back to its character and applicable state. A generic mood image is not a continuity reference unless it resolves an identified shape, feature, material, or state question.

## Expression and pose range

Define readable extremes and neutral or resting behavior from supplied performance needs. Do not equate expression with a human face: nonhuman subjects may communicate through sensor orientation, emissive states, appendage configuration, body compression, scale change, material flex, or pose rhythm. Keep animation timing and acting beats outside this artifact.

## State progression

Number states in story order and link each to the previous state. Describe observable change: garment on/off, hair placement, makeup level, surface contamination, wetness, damage, repair, emission, deformation, or held object relationship. Do not collapse clean, dusty, and damaged into one vague “weathered” state.

Coverage may revisit a prior state only when the story does. Record uncertainty instead of inventing an intermediate state. A reset method belongs to continuity or department planning; the look bible defines the visible target.

## Drift audit

Check for:

- feature mirroring, relocation, count changes, or scale creep;
- changing silhouette or proportion relationships without permission;
- palette or material response drifting between states;
- wardrobe substitution, missing layers, or untracked fastenings;
- hair, makeup, contamination, damage, or wear jumping order;
- expression or pose exceeding the approved subject-specific range;
- references that do not cover the state or angle used by a shot.

Write one project-derived drift rule for each concrete risk. Do not fill the artifact with generic negative prompts.
