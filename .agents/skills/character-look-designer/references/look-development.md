# Look development

Use this reference to turn supplied identity into a bounded visual treatment without inventing personal or biological facts. Describe what a department must preserve and verify, not a vendor workflow.

## Separate canon from rendering

`supplied_identity` records what the source says the character is. `visual_treatment` records how that identity is proposed to read in the project. Keep provenance on both. A supplied occupation, garment, nonhuman type, or observable feature does not authorize invented ethnicity, age, sex, gender, disability, diagnosis, body anatomy, cultural affiliation, or personal history.

Treat `source_context.identity_statements` as a per-character claim ledger. Before marking any nested character, continuity-state, or reference-purpose claim `supplied`, copy both its exact value and its exact `source_reference` into one ledger statement owned by that character. Do not accept a value-only match, a reference-only match, or another character's statement. Use `inferred` or `proposed` when the source does not state the exact claim.

## Proportions and silhouette

Use relationships before numbers: compact relative mass, long-to-short appendage contrast, head-to-torso hierarchy, stance width, or negative space. Add measurements only when supplied or approved. Test silhouette against the smallest supplied framing where recognition must survive.

For nonhuman subjects, describe the relevant structure—shell, chassis, membrane, branches, clustered mass, articulated segments, changing volume, locomotion base, or other supplied form. Do not force face, hair, makeup, hands, or bipedal anatomy. Use `not-applicable` with a sourced explanation where the schema requires an explicit category.

## Face, body, and subject-type descriptors

Prefer observable feature placement, count, form, articulation, and surface boundary. Avoid evaluative body language and stereotypes. For a live-action human, record only supplied or approved appearance facts and bounded design treatment. For an animated nonhuman, focus on recognition, articulation, readable orientation, material behavior, and controlled shape change.

## Palette and material response

Give colors roles rather than inventing exact values: identity base, state signal, separation, contamination, or damage evidence. State whether a value is supplied, proposed, or unknown. Describe material response—matte, reflective, translucent, flexible, rigid, wet, dusty—only when it affects recognition or continuity. Leave exact substrate, finish, shader, product, and fabrication method unresolved.

## Hair, makeup, and wardrobe

Model hair and makeup as observable continuity states: parting, length, arrangement, wetness, disturbance, application, removal, contamination, or injury treatment when supplied. Never use makeup to invent ethnicity, diagnosis, age, sex, gender, injury, or health information.

Model wardrobe by layer, fastening, orientation, fit relationship, material behavior, carried items, cleanliness, wetness, dust, damage, and repairs that appear in supplied scenes. A garment may move with action while still preserving required feature visibility. Do not select brands, sizes, vendors, performers, quantities, purchases, or construction methods.

## Wear and damage progression

Separate deposition from structural change. Dust, mud, water, blood, soot, wrinkles, or scuffs do not imply tears, dents, injury, or failure. Trace each change to the supplied event and preserve its side, extent, order, and later persistence. If exact distribution is absent, request a reference rather than drawing an arbitrary mask.

## Reference decisions

Each reference need answers a specific unresolved question: which side holds an asymmetric feature, how a material reads at distance, how a garment reveals an anchor, what shape range an expression uses, or how a clean state differs from contamination. Record the character, states, view, purpose, and provenance. Do not embed images, URLs, base64, model parameters, credentials, or vendor settings.

## Restraint review

- Can every appearance claim point to supplied evidence or a clearly labelled proposal?
- Are all sensitive identity facts absent unless directly supplied?
- Are human-specific fields explicitly not applicable for a nonhuman subject when needed?
- Are exact ratios, colors, materials, timing, damage, and wear left unknown when unsupported?
- Do wardrobe, hair/makeup, material, and damage states follow the supplied story order?
- Does every proposed detail solve recognition, performance, coverage, or continuity?
