# Sound Delivery Assumptions

## Treat delivery targets as supplied requirements

Record a target loudness, true-peak limit, channel layout, sample rate, codec, stem set, and accessibility package only when the distributor or recipient supplies a current specification. Until then, phrase the target as a delivery assumption and request the governing document. A target is not a measured result and no plan is compliant merely because it names a standard.

## Keep measurement evidence exact

Put observed numeric values in the evidence register with an ID, value, unit, and source reference. Reuse that exact record in an item; do not estimate, convert, or turn an intended target into an observed measurement. If no metering report exists, leave measurements absent and request one after the relevant mix stage.

## Accessibility and mastering handoff

Plan captions/subtitles, transcript, and audio-description coordination according to the supplied release requirements and the material's needs. Name the handoff and the required review, but do not claim the tracks, timing, conformance, or master exist. Mastering begins only after a final mix is supplied for the required human handoff together with the current delivery specification, then verifies the supplied measurable requirements.
