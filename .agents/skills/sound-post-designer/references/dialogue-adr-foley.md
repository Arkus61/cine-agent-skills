# Dialogue, ADR, and Foley

## Dialogue is the listening priority

Assemble dialogue from supplied production sources only after review. Preserve a performance's timing, breaths, overlaps, and usable natural texture when they serve the scene. Describe intended repairs as conditional until the relevant audio establishes a fault; avoid broad processing that damages consonants, timbre, or perspective.

## ADR is a decision, not a default

Use ADR only when reviewed material demonstrates an irreparable intelligibility, sync, technical, or performance problem and the replacement can be matched in context. Record the reason, source evidence, intended perspective, and review question. A request to assess ADR is not an ADR requirement, and a planned cue is not a selected take.

## Foley is performed story information

Plan Foley around supplied action, tactile point of view, and the space's scale: footfalls, cloth, props, body movement, or contact can clarify attention. Place it selectively under dialogue and preserve an action's weight, distance, and material character. Do not list generic footsteps or fabric merely because a scene contains people.

## Handoff questions

- Which production tracks, guide mix, picture reference, timecode, and edit version were supplied?
- Which audible problems are confirmed by review, and which remain to assess?
- What performance or action needs audible clarification without competing with dialogue?
