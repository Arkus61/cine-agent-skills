# Sound Edit, Design, and Mix

## Build a perspective before adding detail

Use room tone and ambience to maintain the space across picture edits. Give beds, hard effects, and designed layers distinct roles, then state whether the audience hears an objective space, a character's selective attention, or an intentional move between them. A subjective transition changes focus, distance, spectrum, density, or dynamics for a dramatic reason; it is not a generic increase in volume.

## Make transitions and silence deliberate

Use a transition to carry continuity, anticipation, interruption, or a shift in point of view. Intentional silence or thinning must name its dramatic purpose and what it releases, withholds, or makes audible. Silence is not a placeholder for missing sound or an unreviewed defect.

## Organize the premix around decisions

Give dialogue, ADR, Foley, ambience, effects, designed sound, music when supplied, and accessibility material separate, reviewable premix ownership. Automate level, density, perspective, or masking around story turns; protect intelligibility before adding impact. Mix priority identifies the listener's primary attention, not a fixed level.

## Review in context

Check each change in isolation and against picture, then disclose unresolved source, sync, edit, and delivery dependencies. Do not label a pass final, approved, or compliant until its relevant supplied specification, measurement, and authorized review exist.
