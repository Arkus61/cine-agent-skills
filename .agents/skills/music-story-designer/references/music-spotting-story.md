# Music spotting and story

Spotting asks what a music decision changes in attention, expectation, memory, pace, or emotional distance. Begin with the edit segment’s dramatic purpose, then decide whether score earns an entrance at all. A cue can prepare a turn, connect separated images, complicate an apparent victory, or let an audience notice absence; it should not merely announce what the picture already states.

Describe entry and exit as editorial logic, not invented timing. Shape energy with proposed density, register, harmonic stability, pulse, and release. A modular commercial cue may offer alternate intensity and edit-point intent; a horror cue may withhold pulse, impact, and resolution. These are proposals until picture, dialogue, and approval evidence establish a different fact.

Spot every registered segment explicitly. An unscored decision is an active choice with a story purpose, not a gap concealed as a future cue. Keep silence available when score would weaken a performance, reveal, physical sound, or necessary uncertainty.
