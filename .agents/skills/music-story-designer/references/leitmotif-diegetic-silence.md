# Leitmotif, diegesis, and silence

A leitmotif is a recurring relationship between musical material and a story function, not a requirement to state a tune whenever a character appears. Bind it only to registered character or theme IDs. Transformation can be proposed through interval, harmony, register, timbre, rhythm, fragmentation, or omission, while preserving the difference between a creative proposal and a recorded or approved result.

Name whether music is diegetic, nondiegetic, or an unresolved source assumption. A radio, performance, or source track is not present, licensed, or cleared merely because the plan proposes one. Do not request imitation of a living artist, or copying, reconstruction, or close resemblance to a copyrighted song or melody. Use functional descriptors such as sparse bowed texture, syncopated pulse, or era-level instrumentation instead.

When dialogue is protected, speech has priority. State how music yields: ducking, thinning, rests, reduced masking, or silence. A cue covering multiple protected segments needs a decision for each; one dialogue note cannot authorize music under another line.
