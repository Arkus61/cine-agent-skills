# Department breakdown reasoning

## Requirements come from the script and approved plan

ScreenSkills describes prop teams marking up scripts, listing what action and set dressing require, and coordinating the resulting work with production design and other departments. The general breakdown principle is to trace a physical or recorded need to story evidence before proposing how it is sourced or built.

Source: [ScreenSkills — Prop master](https://www.screenskills.com/job-profiles/browse/film-and-tv-drama/craft/prop-master/).

## Ownership and communication

ScreenSkills describes production coordinators as communicating with heads of department and distributing changing production information. A useful scene breakdown therefore normalizes categories, points to the evidence, and makes cross-department dependencies reviewable. It is not a replacement for department plans or production management.

Source: [ScreenSkills — Production coordinator](https://www.screenskills.com/job-profiles/browse/film-and-tv-drama/production-management/production-coordinator-film-and-tv-drama/).

## Requirement versus solution

Write `a repeatable chamber opening state that supports the performer and clean passes`, not an invented actuator, supplier, model, or price. If the practical method is undecided, keep the requirement and put the method in assumptions or an `open-question` item.

## Reset

Every item must say what is restored, matched, or logged between takes. Examples include prop position, moisture/frost state, wardrobe disturbance, practical start state, lighting cue state, noise state, eyeline, clean plate geometry, and effect timing.

## Qualitative risk

- `low`: straightforward, repeatable, and isolated; failure has a limited local effect.
- `medium`: needs coordination or testing; failure affects multiple shots or a clear story detail.
- `high`: safety-sensitive, hard to reset, VFX/practical dependent, or critical to the scene's central story turn.

Risk is a review priority, not a cost estimate, schedule, or safety certification.
